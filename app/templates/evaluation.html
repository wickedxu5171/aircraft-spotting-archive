Why human review remains necessary

One registration can return several plausible ADS-B legs

Operational-frequency ambiguity: high-frequency narrow-body aircraft may operate several sectors on the same calendar day. If an observation has only a date, registration agreement can return multiple legitimate ADS-B legs for the same airframe. G-EUPJ and G-NEOU are examples in this archive.

Registration-only candidate ambiguity: the problem is not limited to narrow-body schedules. G-VPRD produced three same-airframe candidates with different callsigns: #555/VIR92MC, #556/VIR103M and #557/VIR104L. All received the same 40 registration points. After the photo date was corrected to 2025-11-05, its 12:08 capture time became valid independent evidence: #556 received 25 time points and 82.5 overall, while #555 received 4 time points and 59 overall and #557 received no time points and 40 overall. The human-reviewed photo metadata therefore resolves the competing candidates rather than registration agreement alone deciding the link.

ADS-B enrichment therefore adds useful callsign, route, time and trajectory evidence while also exposing competing candidates. A ranked result is an explainable hypothesis, not a fact; the final link remains a human decision when decisive independent evidence is missing.

How to complete an independent manual review
  1. Choose a representative subset. The current snapshot contains 25 independently reviewed records from 2025-11-05. Add the four 2026-05-16 records only if cross-date conclusions are required, and keep genuinely unresolved cases unevaluated.
  2. Find evidence independent of this matcher. Prefer the photograph's EXIF capture time, original spotting notes, or a separate historical flight playback/source. Do not treat the top score itself as proof.
  3. Compare each candidate. Check registration, capture-time window, Heathrow proximity, route endpoints and operational callsign. Open the candidate flight page to inspect its trajectory.
  4. Save only a defensible conclusion. Select the verified candidate, or explicitly select “Verified: no candidate matches”. If evidence is insufficient, leave the record unverified and do not submit the form.

In Notes, record the evidence source and decisive facts, for example: “Photo EXIF 14:32 UTC; candidate #123 passed LHR at 14:29; route and registration agree.”

How the score works

Candidate scores are weighted evidence, not percentages

Each candidate is an ADS-B flight leg that could correspond to the spotting event. The total is the sum of five evidence components. A higher score means a stronger automated hypothesis, but it is not proof of a correct match.

Registration
up to 40 points
Time
up to 25 points
Airport
up to 15 points
Callsign
up to 15 points
Route
up to 5 points

Automated classification: 80–100 = Matched; 60–79 = Review; below 60 = Unmatched. The sample-event count refers to the records in the selected scope, not to disputed matches.
